Things nobody tells you

Eight facts that reframe
the whole subject.

You don't need to know anything about astrology to follow these. They are astronomy, history and arithmetic — and together they explain why this system is far stranger, and far more exact, than its reputation suggests. Sanskrit terms appear, but always with the plain meaning first.

01 / PRECESSION
20m 24s

The year has two lengths

A sidereal year — Earth's true orbit, star to star — runs 365.2564 days. The tropical year, equinox to equinox, runs 365.2422. That 20-minute-24-second gap is precession. Every year, the two zodiacs slip a little further apart. Western astrology tracks the shorter one. Vedic tracks the real one.

02 / ASCENDANT
4 min = 1°

Your birth minute is load-bearing

The entire 360° zodiac rises past the horizon in 24 hours — so the ascendant sweeps a full degree every four minutes. Someone born four minutes after you, in the same room, has a measurably different chart. Two hours later and they don't even share your rising sign. This is why "roughly morning" isn't a birth time.

03 / NAVAMSA
13m 20s

There are charts inside your chart

Your birth chart isn't one picture. Each sign gets cut into nine slices to build a second, finer chart — the navamsha — read for marriage and inner character. Sixteen of these nested charts are in normal use: one for career, one for wealth, one for spiritual life. The marriage one changes every 13 minutes 20 seconds, which is one more reason your exact birth time matters.

04 / VIMSHOTTARI
120 yrs

A clock keyed to the Moon

Indian astrology maps a life onto nine planetary chapters that add up to exactly 120 years — an idealised full lifespan. The system is called Vimshottari dasha. Which chapter you start in isn't decided by your birthday but by exactly where the Moon sat at your first breath. Two people born the same day, hours apart, can be living in completely different decades.

05 / RAHU & KETU
18.6 yrs

Two "planets" that aren't there

Rahu and Ketu are the lunar nodes — the two points where the Moon's tilted orbit crosses Earth's orbital plane. Eclipses can only happen near them, which is why myth casts them as a serpent swallowing the Sun. They drift backwards through the zodiac on an 18.6-year cycle. Vedic tradition assigns Rahu an 18-year dasha. Make of that what you will.

06 / ORBITS
29.46

The Saturn return is real orbital mechanics

Saturn takes 29.46 years to circle the Sun, so it arrives back where it started at roughly 29–30, then again near 59. Jupiter's 11.86-year orbit means it changes sign about once a year. The "big life chapters" astrology talks about at those ages aren't invented — they're a planet finishing a lap, arriving on schedule.

07 / ASHTAKAVARGA
337

It has a scoring system

There is a scoring system — Ashtakavarga — that spreads exactly 337 points across the twelve areas of a chart, averaging 28 each. Score 32 in one and that part of life is genuinely well-supported; score 22 and it isn't. You cannot argue the number into being friendlier, which is why it regularly contradicts a flattering reading. That is the whole point of it.

08 / HISTORY
1 roof

Astronomy and astrology were the same job

Kepler cast horoscopes for a living while deriving the laws of planetary motion. Galileo drew charts. The Vedanga Jyotisha — one of the six limbs of the Vedas — is among the oldest surviving astronomical texts on Earth. Predicting where a planet would be and asking what it meant were a single discipline for most of recorded history. The split is recent.

The 1° that outlives you

The ayanamsha grows by one full degree every 71.6 years. Which means the gap your grandparents were born under was measurably smaller than yours, and your great-grandchildren will be born under a wider one. A zodiac tied to the stars keeps working. A zodiac tied to a 2,000-year-old memo does not.

The 27-fold sky

Before there were 12 signs,
there were 27 Moon-houses.

The Moon crosses about 13°20′ of sky a day. Divide the circle by that and you get 27 — the nakshatras, or lunar mansions: the 27 rooms the Moon sleeps in on successive nights. This way of cutting the sky is older than the twelve signs in India, and it does most of the real work. Your life-timing sequence, much of your temperament, and the filters used in marriage matching all key off the room the Moon was in when you were born. Not your Sun sign. Your Moon's address.

Each nakshatra splits further into four padas of 3°20′ — 108 divisions in total. Yes, that 108. Qualities below are classical associations, not verdicts: a nakshatra describes texture and temperament, never a fixed outcome.

Timing

A 120-year clock, started
at your first breath.

A horoscope column tells you what you're like. This tells you when — and that is the real difference. A life divides into nine planetary chapters in a fixed order, adding up to exactly 120 years. Where you enter that sequence depends on the Moon's exact position at birth, so the same chapter arrives at a different age for everyone. When a reading mentions a "window", this is the machinery underneath it.

Each mahadasha subdivides into nine antardashas, each of those into nine pratyantardashas, and so on down five levels. The resolution goes to days. This is where a reading stops being descriptive and starts being about when.

The Sanskrit, decoded · ten words, ten lines
Jyotisha (jyotiṣa)"Science of light" — the Indian name for this whole tradition. Vedic astrology.
GrahaA planet, as astrology counts them. There are nine, and two of them aren't objects.
Rashi (rāśi)A sign — one of the twelve 30° slices of sky. Aries, Taurus, and so on.
Nakshatra (nakṣatra)A finer slice. The sky also divides into 27, one per night the Moon spends crossing it.
LagnaYour rising sign — the exact bit of sky coming up over the eastern horizon as you were born.
Bhava (bhāva)A house. Twelve areas of life — money, home, work, marriage — laid out from your lagna.
Ayanamsha (ayanāṁśa)The correction that fixes the 24° gap between the seasonal zodiac and the real stars.
Dasha (daśā)A chapter of life. Nine of them, in fixed order, timed from where your Moon was.
Navamsha (navāṁśa)A second chart hidden inside the first, read for marriage and inner character.
Dosha (doṣa)A structural difficulty in a chart. Often oversold elsewhere; here it gets checked properly.

Jyotisha keeps its Sanskrit vocabulary because the words are precise — "house" and bhava aren't quite the same idea. So the terms are used throughout this site rather than watered down, and glossed every time they appear. That's genuinely the whole vocabulary you need. The greyed spelling in brackets is the scholarly transliteration you'll meet in translated texts — same word, with the pronunciation marked.

About the tradition

Questions about Jyotisha itself.

Jyotisha (pronounced jyo-ti-sha) means "science of light". It is the Indian astrological tradition — most people in English call it Vedic astrology — and it is one of the six Vedangas, the limbs of the Veda, which is why it sits close to astronomy in Indian thought.

Three things make it different from the Western astrology you've met in magazines. First, the zodiac: Jyotisha uses the sidereal zodiac, fixed to the actual stars, where Western astrology uses the tropical one fixed to the spring equinox — the two have drifted about 24° apart, which is why your sign here is usually one earlier. Second, the Moon matters far more than the Sun; your nakshatra, the Moon's position among 27 divisions, drives most of the reading. Third, and most importantly, Jyotisha has timing systems — the dasha cycles — that say when something is likely to come forward, not just what you are like.
Because the translations leak. Bhava is usually rendered "house", but it literally means a state or mode of being, and it carries connotations that "house" doesn't. Graha gets translated "planet", yet two of the nine grahas — Rahu and Ketu — aren't objects at all, they're the points where the Moon's orbit crosses the Sun's path. Calling them planets is simply wrong.

So the Sanskrit stays, and it is glossed every single time: lagna (rising sign), dasha (life chapter), nakshatra (lunar mansion). You'll pick up ten words over the course of one reading and they'll make everything else legible. There's a short glossary further up this page if you'd like them all in one place.
It isn’t even the same zodiac. Popular horoscopes use the tropical zodiac, fixed to the equinox roughly 2,000 years ago and now drifted about 24° from the actual constellations. This practice uses the sidereal zodiac with the Lahiri ayanamsha, anchored to the real stars. And a horoscope column addresses one twelfth of humanity at once. A chart addresses one birth moment.
